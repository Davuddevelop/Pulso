A first listen, wherever the path leads.

A community health worker's second pair of ears — built from a low-cost contact mic, a deterministic signal pipeline, and hard rules about what it will and will not claim.

Scroll

Trained ears and clinical stethoscopes are scarce exactly where community health workers spend their days.

Pulso listens alongside them. A contact microphone picks up heart sound, a deterministic pipeline finds the beats and reads the rate and rhythm, and the screen says one of three things: normal, review recommended, or signal too noisy.

Nothing more specific than that. No diagnosis, no disease name, no accuracy figure that hasn't actually been measured. The vocabulary is fixed on purpose — a triage flag is useful; a wrong guess dressed up as a finding is not.

Architecture

Everything downstream is ignorant of where the audio came from.

Four stages, each one only aware of the stage before it. A hardware microphone can replace the recorded file later without a single line changing past the first module.

01

sources.py

capture

One contract — fixed-length float32 frames at 2 kHz — for a WAV file, a live microphone, or an in-memory array. A file at any other sample rate is resampled here, once, so no rate ever leaks downstream.

02

dsp.py

filters

Pure functions, no state. A 25–200 Hz bandpass isolates heart sound; a Shannon-energy envelope turns it into something a peak detector can read. Verified first on tones and chirps with a known correct answer.

03

segment.py

beats

Finds S1 and S2 from the timing itself — systole is shorter than diastole — and says so honestly when that asymmetry collapses at a high heart rate, instead of guessing.

04

classify.py + app.py

triage & display

A rate/rhythm heuristic decides the label; the live view draws the waveform, envelope, beat markers and BPM. No DSP logic lives in the display layer.

Engineering

Fixed decisions, and the two that changed on contact with a test.

Sample rate matches the training data so nothing gets resampled on that path. Filter order and envelope method are fixed. Two numbers below differ from the original spec — both because a test built on a signal with a known correct answer caught them before real audio could.

DecisionValueWhy
Internal sample rate2000 HzMatches the CinC 2016 training set exactly — zero resampling on the training path.
Frame size1024~0.5 s of context per frame, still feels real-time.
Heart bandpass25–200 HzButterworth order 4, applied as second-order sections — the direct-form transfer function is numerically unstable at this order.
Lung bandpass100–950 HzChanged from a spec'd 1000 Hz: that value is exactly Nyquist at 2 kHz and isn't a realizable filter edge.
EnvelopeShannon energySmoothed ~50 ms — suppresses low-amplitude noise better than plain rectification.
Beat refractory period80 msChanged from a spec'd 200 ms: measured against a real burst, that suppressed genuine S1–S2 pairs at realistic heart rates, not just double-counted ringing.
Status

What's real, and what's honestly not built yet.

Working

Deterministic pipeline

Capture, filtering, beat detection, rate/rhythm triage, and the live display all run end to end and are covered by 221 automated checks against signals with a known correct answer.

Blocked

Trained classifier

The CNN specified for sound-morphology classification needs the CinC 2016 dataset. PhysioNet is unreachable from this build environment, so it was never trained — not stubbed, simply not attempted.

Unverified

Live microphone path

MicSource is written and unit-tested for its failure modes, but this build environment has no audio device to confirm it against a real contact mic.

Unverified

The display, on a screen

app.py passes a headless smoke test that drives its real update loop, but it has not yet been watched scrolling on an actual monitor.

“

This is a triage flag, not a diagnosis.

Pulso is not a medical device and is not for clinical use. It never outputs a disease name or a treatment suggestion, and it never states an accuracy figure that hasn't been measured on held-out data.

  • Output vocabulary is fixed: normal, review recommended, signal too noisy — nothing else.
  • Built on the PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2016 database.
  • A judge who catches an inflated claim discounts everything else on the slide — so does a health worker relying on the reading.

Citation. Liu C, Springer D, et al. An open access database for the evaluation of heart sound algorithms. Physiol Meas. 2016;37(12):2181–2213. — PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2016.
