Who actually pays

Not the health worker

They don't hold a budget for this. The buyer is the NGO, health ministry, or global-health funder that already equips and funds community health worker programs — the same organizations named in the WHO's own workforce data.

Two revenue lines
  • Hardware sold near bill-of-materials cost — low margin, wide reach, the "accessible" half of the pitch.
  • A program-level reporting subscription, billed to the NGO or ministry, not the individual worker.

Regulatory strategy

Why "not a medical device" is a business decision, not just a safety footnote

The FDA's January 2026 clinical-decision-support guidance exempts software that never processes raw signal-acquisition data from the lightest device-clearance path. Pulso's pipeline filters and analyzes a raw microphone signal directly — so it likely doesn't qualify for that exemption. Staying a non-clinical wellness/triage tool for the pilot phase is a deliberate choice: it reaches NGOs and health programs now, rather than waiting on a multi-year, six-figure clearance process before the first pilot can even start.

Go-to-market

Early stage, honestly

Global-health innovation grants (the kind that already fund community-health-worker tooling) fund the first pilots, not paying customers on day one — that's the normal path in this space, not a weakness to hide. A single NGO pilot validating the software against real, labeled recordings is the next concrete milestone, ahead of any wider rollout.
